## 1 am_liam
I am an approver with a stack of employee requests and a two hundred page policy handbook.

I need answers from our policies that I can trust.

This is TrustDesk, a sample app. The identity provider, the LLM gateway and the vault are reference scaffolding, swappable for your own.

What you are watching is EDB Postgres on the AgentPG platform, doing the work. The vectors live in the same Postgres. There is no external vector database.

## 2 af_heart
First, the rules. We load a fictional company called Acme.

One click adds sixteen policy documents, eleven people, and a set of requests.

[pause 1.5]

Behind the gateway, a model read each request, next to the policy passages that fit. That is all it does. It advises.

Each policy is split into passages. Every passage is stored with a vector, next to its text, in Postgres.

Open one, and you see the passages.

That is the whole ingest. There is no second system.

## 3 am_eric
Now the question I actually have. A new request arrives.

José wants a long weekend. Three days, in eight weeks.

I submit it. The pre-check runs.

Open the full passage, and the sentence is right there.

## 4 af_heart
Now a request that breaks the rules. José asks for fifteen working days, starting in two days.

[pause 1.5]

Suggests rejection. Again, numbered reasons.

Thirty days of notice are needed, and he gave two. Fifteen days need a Director. And one more. The finance blackout does not apply, because José works in Marketing.

Look at the quotes.

Each one carries the green line. That check is not done by the model. TrustDesk compares the quote with the source row in the database. If the words are not there, the line turns amber.

So the model cannot invent a citation.

The AI advises. I decide. I write my reason, and reject.

The same check ran on the live platform, with a real model behind the gateway.

This is a real rejection, captured on the twenty eighth of October. Two days of notice, against thirty. The live policy there asks for VP approval. And the finance blackout does not apply.

Same green line, on the live database.

## 5 am_liam
Now, finding things. Search the policies in plain language.

Each result shows its ranks. One from the vector, one from the exact words.

Now I misspell a word on purpose. Encyption.

[pause 1.0]

No exact word matches. But look at the badge. Typo-tolerant. That is the pg_trgm extension, a third signal, in the same Postgres.

A real embedding model ranks better. This demo embedder only matches words, so take this as the floor.

Names work too. Type alvares, with a typo and no accent, and it finds José Álvarez.

## 6 af_heart
Status is live. An application, here an HR portal, sends a request.

It appears in my list. I did not refresh.

Then someone else decides it, and the status changes on my screen.

This is the tcn extension. Postgres itself tells the app that a row changed.

One honest note. On AgentPG today, the platform ingress does not flush server-sent events. So the browser falls back to polling, after six seconds. Same screen, a little slower.

## 8 af_heart
Last, safety. The AI should never learn who people are.

Here is the read-only database user that an AI connects with.

Ask for the employees view, and names and e-mails are masked.

Ask for the real table, and the database says no.

The masking lives in the database, not in the app.

One finding for the platform team. A new database user on AgentPG re-opens the grants. So the app closes them again, every five minutes.
